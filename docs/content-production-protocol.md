# CrosstownOS Content Production Protocol

## 1. Title

This document is the CrosstownOS Content Production Protocol. It is the standing production standard for research-based content published under the CrosstownOS name.

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this protocol is to make every factual claim CrosstownOS publishes traceable to a source a reader can check.

CrosstownOS teaches verification. Its own content is held to the same test it teaches: a claim needs a source, a number needs a date, and research that was done must be visible on the page. Research that exists only in an internal file does not count as grounding for publication.

This protocol extends, and does not replace, the Editorial Integrity and AI-Assistance Standard adopted on October 5, 2026, and the fact-check rules in the Field Notes Editorial Roadmap. Where an earlier standard states a principle, this protocol states how that principle is checked before publication.

## 3. Scope

This protocol applies to:

- Field Notes.
- Guides, including pillar and investing guides.
- Comparison pages, including any page that names a competitor product or company.
- Any other research-heavy content, meaning any piece that makes market, legal, financial, operational, or comparative claims a reader could rely on in a decision.

This protocol applies to a piece regardless of when the piece was first published. Republication, campaign linking, and the creation of a structured-data version of a piece all count as publication for the purposes of this protocol.

Short-form copy that makes no factual claim outside the scope above is not governed by this protocol. If a short-form piece repeats a number, a legal statement, or a competitor claim from a governed piece, the claim must satisfy this protocol in the governed piece first.

## 4. Definitions

**Load-bearing claim:** A factual claim that the argument, a recommendation, or a reader decision depends on. If removing the claim or proving it false would change what the piece teaches or what a reader would do, the claim is load-bearing. Load-bearing claims include numbers and ranges, statements of law, statements about a named competitor, statements about how buyers, sellers, brokers, lenders, or operators typically behave, and any frequency or superlative claim.

**Research dossier:** The research file assembled before drafting begins for a piece. The dossier contains the sources relied on, identified by name, date, and a location a checker can reach, together with the notes that connect each source to the claims it supports. A dossier is not a bibliography assembled after the fact from memory. It is the record of research actually done before the draft was written.

**Claims ledger:** The list of every load-bearing claim in a piece, attached to the piece's record. Each entry states the claim, its source, and its status. The ledger is started before drafting, maintained while drafting, and checked at the pre-publication gate. A claim with no entry in the ledger does not publish.

**Claim status:** Each ledger entry carries exactly one of the following statuses:

- Grounded: the claim is supported by a named, dated, locatable source recorded in the dossier.
- Consistent-but-uncited: the claim is consistent with known sources or internal research, but no locatable citation is attached yet. A claim in this status does not publish as a statement of fact. It must be grounded, rewritten as a labeled judgment, converted to a labeled working assumption where Article 3 permits, or cut.
- Labeled illustration: the claim, example, or figure is synthetic, constructed to teach a pattern, and is labeled as illustrative in the published piece under Article 6.
- Working assumption: the claim or figure is a CrosstownOS working assumption under the definition below and is printed as one under Article 3.

**Working assumption:** A figure or premise CrosstownOS uses for planning or teaching, stated on the page as a CrosstownOS working assumption, with the method behind it stated and an as-of date given. A working assumption is not presented as market data, is not stated in the past tense as a transaction result, and is not a substitute for a source where a source exists and can be obtained.

**Labeled illustration:** A synthetic example, scenario, composite, or worked example created to demonstrate a method or pattern. A labeled illustration is identified as illustrative at its first appearance in the piece, not later in the piece and not only in a closing note.

**Sources and Limits section:** The section required by Article 9, published with the piece, that names the sources the piece relies on and states plainly what the piece does not know, did not check, or could not establish.

**Flag:** A defect recorded against a claim or a piece by an internal audit, a review, or the pre-publication gate, including an ungrounded claim, a missing citation, a missing jurisdiction, an unlabeled illustration, or a missing required section.

**Structured-data twin:** A structured version of content from a piece, including schema markup, summary tables prepared for extraction, and machine-readable restatements of the piece's claims.

## 5. Articles

### Article 1. Research Dossier Required Before Drafting

1.1. No Field Note and no Guide is drafted before a research dossier exists for it. The same rule applies to comparison pages and other research-heavy content within the scope of this protocol.

1.2. The dossier is assembled first. Drafting follows the dossier, not the reverse. Sources found after drafting begins are added to the dossier and to the claims ledger before the piece proceeds.

1.3. A piece has no draft status, review status, or publication date until its dossier exists and is attached to the piece's record.

### Article 2. Claims Ledger Required

2.1. Every piece within scope carries a claims ledger, attached to the piece's record.

2.2. The ledger lists every load-bearing claim in the piece. Each entry states the claim, its source, and its status as defined in Section 4.

2.3. A claim with no source and no permitted status does not enter the draft. A claim discovered in a draft with no ledger entry is added to the ledger and resolved under this protocol, or cut, before the piece proceeds.

2.4. No piece publishes without its dossier and its ledger attached to the piece's record. This is a hard gate, not a preference.

### Article 3. Numbers Rule

3.1. No number publishes without a source and an as-of date, stated on the page or in the piece's Sources and Limits section.

3.2. A benchmark range requires a named dataset, a geography, and a period. A range without all three does not publish as a benchmark.

3.3. A past-tense market claim, including any claim that properties have traded, sold, or priced at a stated level, requires evidence from closed transactions. Asking prices, listing data, and broker opinion do not satisfy this rule, and the distinction between asking data and closed-transaction data is stated on the page where either is used.

3.4. If no source exists for a figure, the figure is either cut or printed as a labeled CrosstownOS working assumption, with its method stated and an as-of date given, as defined in Section 4.

3.5. No source is invented, inferred, or restated from a page CrosstownOS itself published. A figure whose only source is an earlier CrosstownOS page has no source for the purposes of this article.

### Article 4. Law Rule

4.1. A statement of law names its jurisdiction and cites the instrument relied on: the ordinance, statute, regulation, or agency document, identified so a reader can locate it.

4.2. A legal claim without a named jurisdiction does not publish.

4.3. Where the law varies by jurisdiction and the piece is not written for one named jurisdiction, the piece states that the rule varies by jurisdiction and cites at least one named jurisdiction as an example, or the claim is cut.

4.4. Nothing in a piece governed by this protocol is presented as legal advice. Where a legal question is fact-specific, the piece says so and directs the reader to qualified counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.

### Article 5. Competitor Rule

5.1. A claim about a named competitor cites the competitor's own current material, identified so a reader can locate it.

5.2. Every named-competitor claim carries an as-of date, stated on the page or in the Sources and Limits section.

5.3. Before a piece contrasts a competitor, it concedes the competitor's strongest documented feature, stated fairly and in the competitor's own terms where practical.

5.4. An absolute negative about a named product requires a citation for that specific claim. One citation does not cover a different claim about the same product.

5.5. Two or more distinct products are not described as one undifferentiated category when the piece makes claims a reader could apply to each of them separately.

### Article 6. Illustration Rule

6.1. A synthetic example, scenario, composite, or worked example is labeled as illustrative at its first appearance in the piece.

6.2. Numbers from a labeled illustration are quarantined. They are not stated elsewhere, in the same piece or in any other piece, as benchmarks, market figures, or observed results.

6.3. Numbers from a labeled illustration are not given a structured-data twin as if they were data.

6.4. A composite drawn from real operator input is labeled as a composite. Operator input used in a piece is recorded by name in the claims ledger and is anonymized in print only where the standing editorial boundary on permission and attribution requires it.

### Article 7. Universal Rule

7.1. A frequency claim or a superlative claim, including claims using words such as every, always, never, rarely, least, most, first, or only, requires evidence recorded in the dossier and cited under Article 9.

7.2. A claim of that kind without evidence is rewritten as what it is, a labeled judgment, stated as CrosstownOS judgment, or it is cut.

7.3. Drafting assumes a hostile and informed reader can produce a counterexample. A claim that a single counterexample would falsify is not stated as a universal fact without evidence that survives that test.

7.4. An experience claim is stated only where the experience is real and recorded. A piece does not assert, by wording or by implication, experience the founder has not had.

### Article 8. Four-Domain Check

8.1. Before publication, each piece is checked for presence across four domains: regulatory, operational, financial, and field observation.

8.2. A missing domain is handled in one of two ways, and no other way: the domain is filled with grounded material, or its absence is recorded as a conscious decision in the claims ledger, with the reason stated.

8.3. The four-domain check is recorded in the ledger. An unrecorded check did not happen for the purposes of the pre-publication gate.

### Article 9. Sources and Limits Section Required

9.1. Every research-heavy piece, including every Field Note, Guide, and comparison page, publishes with a Sources and Limits section. There are no exceptions by piece age, piece type within scope, or prior publication.

9.2. The section names the sources the piece relies on, with enough detail for a reader to locate each source, and states the as-of dates required by Articles 3 and 5.

9.3. The section states what the piece does not know: the claims that rest on judgment, the domains consciously omitted under Article 8, the limits of the data used, and any material question the research did not answer.

9.4. A warning, caveat, or disclaimer does not substitute for a source. A piece that can only warn the reader about a figure, and cannot source it, resolves the figure under Article 3.4.

### Article 10. AI-Assistance Disclosure Required

10.1. Every piece within scope publishes with an AI-assistance disclosure footer, in the standing form set by the Editorial Integrity and AI-Assistance Standard.

10.2. The footer is present on every piece, without exception by piece age or prior publication. A piece missing the footer is incomplete and does not pass the pre-publication gate.

### Article 11. Provenance Travels

11.1. Grounding that exists in internal files is cited in the piece's Sources and Limits section. If the research was done, the reader sees it.

11.2. Internal grounding that is not cited on the page counts as no grounding for publication purposes.

11.3. Where an internal source cannot be cited publicly, for example operator input protected by the standing editorial boundary, the ledger records the internal source and the piece cites the basis in the most specific form that boundary permits, such as the type of source and the date of the research.

### Article 12. Flag-to-Fix Rule

12.1. When an audit, a review, or the pre-publication gate flags a claim, the flag attaches to the piece's claims ledger and stays there until it is cleared.

12.2. A flagged piece is not republished, is not linked in a campaign, and is not given a structured-data twin until every flag attached to it is cleared.

12.3. A flag is cleared only by fixing the underlying claim: grounding it, rewriting it under the article that governs it, or cutting it. Recording a flag, noting a flag, or adding a caveat does not clear it.

12.4. A flag is not carried forward silently. An open flag is visible in the piece's record to anyone checking the piece's status.

### Article 13. Structured-Data and Republication Discipline

13.1. No claim receives a structured-data twin before the claim itself passes this protocol.

13.2. A structured-data twin carries the same status as its source claim. A working assumption stays labeled as a working assumption in structured form. A labeled illustration never becomes structured data under Article 6.3.

13.3. Correcting a claim in the body of a piece requires checking and, where needed, correcting every structured-data twin, summary, and internal link description that restates the claim.

### Article 14. No Publication Without the Record

14.1. No piece within scope publishes without its research dossier and its claims ledger attached to the piece's record.

14.2. No piece within scope publishes without its Sources and Limits section and its AI-assistance disclosure footer in place.

14.3. If any item in Section 6 cannot be checked, the piece does not publish. The piece waits. The gate does not.

## 6. Pre-Publication Gate Checklist

Every item below is checked for the specific piece, and the completed checklist is kept with the piece's record. An unchecked item blocks publication.

Research record:

- [ ] A research dossier exists for this piece, was assembled before drafting began, and is attached to the piece's record.
- [ ] A claims ledger exists for this piece and is attached to the piece's record.
- [ ] Every load-bearing claim in the final draft appears in the ledger.
- [ ] Every ledger entry states a source and carries exactly one status: grounded, consistent-but-uncited, labeled illustration, or working assumption.
- [ ] No claim carries the consistent-but-uncited status in the published text as a statement of fact. Each such claim has been grounded, rewritten as a labeled judgment, converted to a labeled working assumption where permitted, or cut.

Numbers:

- [ ] Every number in the piece has a source and an as-of date, on the page or in the Sources and Limits section.
- [ ] Every benchmark range names its dataset, its geography, and its period.
- [ ] Every past-tense market claim rests on closed-transaction evidence, and the asking-versus-closed distinction is stated where either kind of data is used.
- [ ] Every unsourced figure has been cut, or is printed as a labeled CrosstownOS working assumption with its method and as-of date stated.
- [ ] No figure is sourced only to an earlier CrosstownOS page.

Law:

- [ ] Every statement of law names a jurisdiction and cites the ordinance, statute, regulation, or agency document relied on.
- [ ] Every jurisdiction-sensitive claim that is not written for one named jurisdiction states that the rule varies by jurisdiction, with at least one named example cited, or has been cut.
- [ ] No part of the piece reads as legal advice, and fact-specific legal questions are referred to qualified counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.

Competitors:

- [ ] Every claim about a named competitor cites that competitor's own current material and carries an as-of date.
- [ ] The competitor's strongest documented feature is conceded, fairly stated, before any contrast is drawn.
- [ ] Every absolute negative about a named product has its own citation.
- [ ] No two distinct products are treated as one undifferentiated category where the claims could be applied to each separately.

Illustrations and universals:

- [ ] Every synthetic example, scenario, composite, and worked example is labeled as illustrative at its first appearance.
- [ ] No number from a labeled illustration appears anywhere as a benchmark, market figure, or observed result, and no such number has a structured-data twin.
- [ ] Every frequency or superlative claim is supported by evidence in the dossier and cited, or has been rewritten as a labeled judgment, or has been cut.
- [ ] No experience claim states or implies experience the founder has not had and recorded.

Coverage and disclosure:

- [ ] The four-domain check (regulatory, operational, financial, field observation) is recorded in the ledger.
- [ ] Every missing domain has been filled, or its absence is recorded in the ledger as a conscious decision with the reason stated.
- [ ] The Sources and Limits section is present, names the sources relied on with their as-of dates, and states what the piece does not know.
- [ ] Internal grounding relied on is cited in the Sources and Limits section in the most specific form the editorial boundary permits.
- [ ] The AI-assistance disclosure footer is present in the standing form.

Flags and structured data:

- [ ] No open flag is attached to this piece in its ledger.
- [ ] No structured-data twin restates a claim that has not passed this checklist.
- [ ] Every structured-data twin, summary, and internal link description restating a corrected claim has been checked and corrected with it.

## 7. Administration

7.1. The dossier, the ledger, and the completed gate checklist are kept with the piece's record for as long as the piece is published. The site-wide claims ledger for published pages is maintained at docs/claims-ledger.md in this repository and is the enforcement record for this protocol across the live site; per-piece ledgers reconcile to it at publication.

7.2. An amendment to this protocol is made in writing, recorded with its date, and applies to pieces published or republished after the amendment takes effect.

7.3. A conflict between this protocol and an earlier internal standard is resolved in favor of the stricter requirement, until the founder resolves the conflict in writing.
